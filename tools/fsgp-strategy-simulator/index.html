Rules in the model

What the model assumes

Sun position is computed for Gravois Mills, Missouri (38.27° N) in mid-July, with a clear-sky irradiance curve dimmed by each day's cloud cover. Array output is area × cell efficiency × 0.93 for wiring, tracker and heat losses.

Car power is aerodynamic drag plus rolling resistance plus braking losses on the corners and hills you set, divided by drivetrain efficiency, plus 25 W of electronics. Regenerative braking returns half of the corner energy. Battery charge and discharge are each 97% efficient.

The car starts day 1 with a full battery. If the battery empties, the car slows to the speed the array can sustain. Below 10 mph it parks and charges to 15% before rejoining. Laps are counted per day as whole laps completed before the checkered flag. Reliability, traffic, tire changes and penalties are not modelled, so a real team will score lower.

Rules come from the ASC/FSGP 2026 Regulations, Revision B and the FSGP 2025 Regulations, Release C, both published by the Innovators Educational Foundation. Check the current revision before relying on a number. Tilting the array on stands is standard charging practice and is treated here as allowed.

Built for SERRT, the Solar Engineering Research Racing Team at the University of Puerto Rico, Mayagüez. Car defaults are example values for a typical single-occupant car, not a specific vehicle.
